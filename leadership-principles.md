# Amazon Leadership Principles Interview Guide

Amazon interviews use behavioral questions to understand how you have acted in prior situations. Prepare specific, metric-backed stories and deliver them with **STAR**:

- **Situation** — the context and stakes (briefly).
- **Task** — your responsibility or goal.
- **Action** — what *you* did, how you made decisions, and who you involved.
- **Result** — the measurable outcome, what changed, and what you learned.

Use one story for more than one principle only when it genuinely demonstrates both. Aim for 6–8 flexible stories across projects, failures, conflict, customer impact, ambiguity, and leadership.

> Source: [Amazon Leadership Principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles). The summaries and interview guidance below are study notes, not official wording.

## 1. Customer Obsession

**What to demonstrate:** Start with the customer problem, use evidence to understand it, and earn trust through an outcome that matters to them.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about a time you advocated for a customer despite competing priorities.
- Describe a time customer feedback changed your approach.

**Story checklist:** Who was the customer? How did you learn what they needed? What tradeoff did you make? What customer metric or feedback improved?

## 2. Ownership

**What to demonstrate:** Take responsibility beyond your formal scope; protect long-term value rather than optimizing only your immediate task.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about a problem that was not your responsibility but that you solved.
- Describe a time you took ownership of a difficult outcome.

**Story checklist:** What gap did you notice? Why did it matter beyond your team? How did you sustain the fix after the immediate issue passed?

## 3. Invent and Simplify

**What to demonstrate:** Challenge an unnecessarily complex status quo, bring in useful ideas, and create a simpler or more scalable path.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about a time you simplified a process.
- Describe an idea you introduced that people initially resisted or misunderstood.

**Story checklist:** What made the old approach costly or confusing? What alternatives did you test? What became simpler—time, cost, steps, or risk?

## 4. Are Right, A Lot

**What to demonstrate:** Use sound judgment under uncertainty, seek opposing views, and update your position when evidence proves you wrong.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about a difficult decision you made with incomplete information.
- Describe a time someone challenged your judgment and what you did next.

**Story checklist:** What evidence informed the decision? Whose perspective did you seek? What was the decision mechanism and result?

## 5. Learn and Be Curious

**What to demonstrate:** Proactively learn, explore unfamiliar territory, and apply that learning to create a better result.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about a skill you taught yourself to solve a problem.
- Describe a time curiosity led you to uncover an opportunity or risk.

**Story checklist:** What triggered the learning? How did you learn quickly? How did the new knowledge change your actions or outcome?

## 6. Hire and Develop the Best

**What to demonstrate:** Raise the bar through hiring, coaching, feedback, mentoring, or creating mechanisms that help people grow.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about someone you coached to improve performance or reach a goal.
- Describe how you made a hiring or team-quality decision.

**Story checklist:** What was the person’s starting point? What specific feedback or support did you provide? What sustained improvement resulted?

## 7. Insist on the Highest Standards

**What to demonstrate:** Set a high, explicit quality bar; catch defects early; and build prevention rather than accepting recurring problems.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about a time you improved quality or prevented a defect from reaching customers.
- Describe when you pushed back because work was not ready to ship.

**Story checklist:** What standard was at risk? How did you validate quality? What permanent mechanism prevented repetition?

## 8. Think Big

**What to demonstrate:** Connect near-term work to a bold customer or business opportunity; articulate a scalable, inspiring direction.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about a time you proposed a larger vision than the original request.
- Describe a time you found an opportunity others had overlooked.

**Story checklist:** What was the long-term opportunity? How did you make the vision concrete? What first step made it credible?

## 9. Bias for Action

**What to demonstrate:** Move quickly on reversible decisions, manage risk deliberately, and avoid analysis paralysis.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about a time you made a fast decision with limited data.
- Describe a calculated risk you took.

**Story checklist:** Why was speed important? What made the decision reversible or safe enough? What guardrails did you use?

## 10. Frugality

**What to demonstrate:** Deliver meaningful results with constrained resources by prioritizing, reusing, automating, or designing creatively.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about a time you delivered under a tight budget, timeline, or staffing constraint.
- Describe a resourceful solution you created.

**Story checklist:** What constraint mattered? What did you decide not to do? What was saved or enabled without compromising the needed outcome?

## 11. Earn Trust

**What to demonstrate:** Communicate candidly and respectfully, listen, admit mistakes, and follow through consistently.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about a time you rebuilt trust after a mistake or difficult relationship.
- Describe a time you delivered hard feedback.

**Story checklist:** What did trust look like in this situation? How did you listen? What did you own personally? How did you know trust improved?

## 12. Dive Deep

**What to demonstrate:** Understand the details, verify assumptions with data, and connect operational signals to the root cause.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about a time you used data to identify the root cause of a problem.
- Describe a time metrics and anecdotal feedback did not agree.

**Story checklist:** What data did you inspect? How did you rule out plausible causes? What detailed insight changed the decision?

## 13. Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit

**What to demonstrate:** Challenge a decision respectfully with evidence, then fully support and execute the final decision once made.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about a time you disagreed with a decision or leader.
- Describe a decision you disagreed with but still had to implement.

**Story checklist:** What was at stake? How did you voice the concern constructively? Once decided, what did commitment look like in practice?

## 14. Deliver Results

**What to demonstrate:** Focus on the critical inputs, manage setbacks, and complete valuable work with the expected quality and timing.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about a difficult goal you delivered despite obstacles.
- Describe a time you had to recover a project that was off track.

**Story checklist:** What was the goal and deadline? Which inputs did you control? How did you adapt? What were the results and quality measures?

## 15. Strive to be Earth’s Best Employer

**What to demonstrate:** Create an environment where people are safe, included, empowered, and able to grow.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about a time you made a team more inclusive, safe, or effective.
- Describe how you helped someone grow or prepare for their next opportunity.

**Story checklist:** Whose experience was improving? What barrier did you remove? What evidence shows people were safer, more capable, or more empowered?

## 16. Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility

**What to demonstrate:** Consider second-order effects on customers, communities, partners, and the future; leave systems better than you found them.

**Practice prompts**

- Tell me about a time you identified an unintended consequence before or after launch.
- Describe a time you made a decision with broader stakeholder impact in mind.

**Story checklist:** Who was affected beyond the immediate user or team? What tradeoffs did you surface? How did your action reduce harm or create durable value?

## Answer quality check

Before practicing an answer, confirm that you can answer “yes” to each:

- Is the example real, specific, and recent enough to discuss fluently?
- Is my individual contribution unmistakable (use “I,” not only “we”)?
- Did I explain the reasoning behind key choices and tradeoffs?
- Did I include numbers, scope, or a clear observable result where possible?
- Did I acknowledge a setback, limitation, or lesson when it belongs in the story?
- Can I adapt the same core story to a related question without forcing the fit?

## A simple practice routine

1. Choose one principle and one story from the story bank.
2. Deliver a two-minute STAR answer aloud; keep the situation and task to about 20% of the time.
3. Ask: “What did you personally do?” and “What would you do differently?” Answer both.
4. Add a metric, decision tradeoff, or lesson if either answer feels vague.
5. Repeat with a different principle until each principle has at least one credible story.
