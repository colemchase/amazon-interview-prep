# Deadline

**Leadership Principles:** Deliver Results; Bias for Action  
**Use:** [Delivering a Go-Live When an Integration Failed at the Last Minute](../core-stories/last-minute-integration-recovery.md)

## Draft answer

> Under a deadline, I focus first on the outcome that cannot move, then separate it from the parts of the plan that can change. For example, I was five minutes from a go-live when an integration on another team’s side failed. I did not assume the deadline meant we should take an unsafe risk, and I did not wait passively for the original integration to be fixed. I proposed a temporary solution that safely supported the launch, made sure everyone understood it was temporary, and then followed through after go-live until the underlying issue was closed. That is how I try to work under pressure: protect the important outcome, make the tradeoffs explicit, and keep ownership of any debt created by the short-term decision.

## What to emphasize

- A deadline does not lower the safety or quality bar; it changes how you sequence work.
- Describe the decision-making, not only the urgency.
- Show that temporary measures always have an owner and a closure path.
