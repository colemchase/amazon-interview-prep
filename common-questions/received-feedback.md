# Tell Me About a Time You Received Feedback

**Leadership Principles:** Bias for Action; Earn Trust; Deliver Results

**Use:** [Using Feedback to Turn Stalled Improvements into Progress](../core-stories/incremental-improvement-through-feedback.md)

## Draft answer

> In my current role, I would often bring a solution for a problem I had found to standup. Sometimes the technical lead preferred a more comprehensive solution and wanted to incorporate my fix later. The bigger solution was often better in an ideal world, but it needed more coordination and sometimes did not get scheduled. The smaller issue would then remain unresolved.
>
> My career manager gave me useful feedback: coming with solutions was good, but I needed to distinguish the ideal future state from the next useful step. They suggested that, when the conversation moved toward a later comprehensive solution, I should say: “Yes, ideally we can continue down that path. For now, let’s take a step in the right direction in case we otherwise take no steps at all.”
>
> I adopted that approach. I acknowledged the value of the broader design, proposed a safe smaller improvement now, and documented the larger solution for later coordination. This helped me resolve issues that would otherwise have stayed open while preserving the path to the more complete solution. Over time, those small wins removed recurring problems and created more headroom for larger initiatives. The feedback taught me how to balance progress with idealism.

## What the interviewer is listening for

- You seek and accept candid feedback without becoming defensive.
- You turn feedback into a specific behavior change.
- You can show evidence that the change worked.

## Skim notes

- Standup solutions stall.
- Ideal solution deferred.
- Manager gives feedback.
- Separate now, later.
- Deliver safe increments.
- Backlog comprehensive design.
- Create team headroom.

## Details to add before the interview

- Which standup discussion and smaller fix best demonstrate the feedback?
- What did the technical lead’s ideal design involve?
- How did you show the incremental fix was safe and compatible with the future design?
- How did you record and revisit the larger solution?
- What recurring issue was removed, and what capacity did that create?

## Likely follow-ups

- How did you respond when you first received the feedback?
- Why did the smaller fix not create technical debt?
- How did you decide whether to act now or wait for the comprehensive solution?
- What evidence showed the new approach was working?
