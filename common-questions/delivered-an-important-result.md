# Tell Me About a Time You Delivered an Important Result Under Pressure

**Leadership Principle:** Deliver Results  
**Use:** [Delivering a Go-Live When an Integration Failed at the Last Minute](../core-stories/last-minute-integration-recovery.md)

## Draft answer

> I was responsible for a go-live that depended on an integration with another team. Five minutes before launch, we found that the integration was not working on their side. Waiting for a permanent fix would have missed the deadline, but I did not want to launch by ignoring the problem. I proposed a temporary solution that allowed us to launch safely and was clear about its limitations. Once the go-live was protected, I followed up with the other team until the underlying integration issue was closed. We met the deadline, and I made sure the temporary path did not become an unowned permanent workaround.

## Skim notes

- External integration dependency.
- Five-minute launch failure.
- Propose safe workaround.
- Launch with transparency.
- Close permanent fix.
- Deadline safely protected.

## What to emphasize

- Define success as meeting the launch commitment safely, not just declaring the integration fixed.
- Explain the judgment behind the temporary solution and its limitations.
- The follow-through matters: the launch was not the end of the work.

## Details to confirm

- What was launching and why was the deadline important?
- What was the temporary solution, and why was it safe enough to use?
- Who approved the approach, and how did you communicate the remaining risk?
- How did the permanent integration fix get tested and closed?

## Likely follow-ups

- How did you decide the temporary path was safe?
- What would you have done if there had been no safe workaround?
- How did you make sure the integration issue did not get forgotten after launch?
