# Tell Me About a Time You Disagreed with a Decision and Then Committed

**Leadership Principle:** Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit  
**Use:** [Creating Capacity for a Platform Team](../core-stories/platform-capacity-transformation.md)

## Draft answer

> When I joined a platform project, operations and service tickets consumed the team’s capacity. Leadership’s view was that keeping the lights on had to take precedence, so a larger migration could not be approved. I disagreed with the idea that this meant the migration should remain out of reach indefinitely, but I agreed that the immediate operational work mattered. Instead of continuing to argue for the migration, I committed to solving the capacity constraint first. I automated and simplified my recurring maintenance work, delivered smaller improvements that reduced team overhead, and used the resulting capacity to develop a migration plan. Each phase created evidence that made the next decision easier. The team eventually had capacity for larger platform work, and I obtained approval to plan and execute migrations independently. I disagreed with the permanent conclusion, but fully committed to the immediate priority and built the path forward.

## Skim notes

## What to emphasize

- Distinguish the decision you accepted from the assumption you challenged.
- Show that commitment meant helping solve the constraint, not withdrawing support.
- Avoid characterizing leadership as irrational; focus on competing priorities.

## Details to confirm

- What migration was later approved and its outcome.
- How much capacity the maintenance automation or smaller initiatives created.
- How you communicated progress and decision points to leadership.

## Likely follow-ups

- Why did you not propose the large migration immediately?
- What would you have done if approval never came?
- How did you maintain the team’s operational commitments while creating capacity?
