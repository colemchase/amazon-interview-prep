# Tell Me About a Time You Earned Trust

**Leadership Principle:** Earn Trust  
**Use:** [Earning Trust to Lead a Larger CI/CD Migration](../core-stories/earning-trust-through-cicd-delivery.md)

## Draft answer

> I wanted to lead a CI/CD migration, but my technical lead was understandably hesitant to give me a large, high-impact initiative before I had established credibility with the team. Instead of pushing the proposal repeatedly, I deepened my knowledge of the stack in my own time and looked for a smaller, measurable way to contribute. I fixed an infrastructure-as-code pipeline issue and reduced its build and deployment time by 50 percent. That led to the opportunity to deliver a smaller initiative successfully. I used those results to communicate the larger migration more concretely—its value, risk, and implementation path. Over time, the sequence of preparation and delivery earned me the trust to move the larger migration forward. It taught me that trust is built through reliable outcomes and clear communication, not by asking others to take a leap of faith.

## Skim notes

- Migration needs trust.
- Learn stack independently.
- Halve IaC pipeline.
- Deliver smaller initiative.
- Clarify migration path.
- Earn through delivery.

## What to emphasize

- Acknowledge the lead’s concern as reasonable; do not frame it as unfair resistance.
- Show the sequence from preparation, to measurable result, to broader scope.
- End with how you changed stakeholder confidence.

## Details to confirm

- What caused the infrastructure-as-code slowdown and the before/after times.
- The smaller initiative and the evidence that it succeeded.
- The technical lead’s original concerns and what specifically resolved them.

## Likely follow-ups

- How did you avoid becoming defensive when you were not initially trusted?
- How did you make sure the 50 percent improvement would last?
- What did you change in your communication before receiving approval?
