# Earning Trust to Lead a Larger CI/CD Migration

## Leadership Principle demonstrated

- [Earn Trust](../leadership-principles/earn-trust.md) — primary

## STAR story

### Situation

I wanted to implement a new CI/CD pipeline, but the technical lead was hesitant to entrust me with that larger initiative. I had an idea for the migration, but I needed to establish credibility with the team before asking for approval to lead it.

### Task

Build the technical confidence and stakeholder trust needed to take on a larger CI/CD migration, while first delivering value within the existing platform.

### Action

I used my free time to deepen my understanding of the team’s technology stack. That work built my technical confidence and gave me a better foundation for making sound changes in the existing environment.

I then addressed an existing infrastructure-as-code pipeline problem and reduced its build and deployment time by half. With that result, I earned the opportunity to lead a smaller initiative, which was also successful.

Rather than repeatedly presenting the larger migration as a single big proposal, I used what I learned from these deliveries to communicate the migration more clearly: its value, implementation approach, risk, and path to approval. This helped me gain support for the larger idea.

### Result

I reduced infrastructure-as-code pipeline build and deployment time by **50%**. More importantly, I established credibility through a sequence of successful deliveries, progressing from an existing fix to a smaller initiative and then toward approval for the larger CI/CD migration.

## Why this story fits

**Earn Trust:** I did not expect trust based only on an idea. I built it through preparation, a measurable improvement, consistent delivery, and clearer communication of the larger proposal.

## Impact metrics

- **Performance:** Reduced infrastructure-as-code pipeline build and deployment time by **half**.
- **Trust and scope:** Earned the opportunity to lead a smaller successful initiative and communicate the larger migration in a way that gained approval.

## Details to add before the interview

- What about the stack did you learn, and how did it change your approach?
- What was wrong with the infrastructure-as-code pipeline before the fix?
- What were the build and deployment times before and after the change?
- What was the smaller initiative, and what made it successful?
- Who needed to approve the larger migration, and what concerns did they have?
- What changed in your proposal or communication that made approval possible?

## Likely follow-ups

- Why did the technical lead hesitate initially?
- How did you respond to the lack of trust without becoming defensive?
- What did you do to ensure the 50% improvement was sustainable?
- How did you decide the smaller initiative was the right next step?
- What did you learn about earning trust on a technical team?
