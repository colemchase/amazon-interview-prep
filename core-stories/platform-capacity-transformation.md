# Creating Capacity for a Platform Team to Move Beyond KTLO

## Leadership Principles demonstrated

- [Think Big](../leadership-principles/think-big.md) — primary
- [Ownership](../leadership-principles/ownership.md)
- [Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit](../leadership-principles/have-backbone-disagree-and-commit.md)

## STAR story

### Situation

When I first joined the platform project, I was assigned operations-and-maintenance work and service tickets for developer requests. The reactive workload consumed the available time, making it difficult to keep up with either responsibility. Larger improvements and migrations were effectively out of reach because the team did not have capacity to prioritize them. Leadership’s view was that KTLO work had to take precedence, so a larger migration could not be approved at that time.

### Task

Create a practical path for the team to move from reactive KTLO work toward larger platform improvements, while continuing to support developers and maintain the existing environment.

### Action

I created a three-phase plan:

1. **Free up my own capacity.** I simplified and automated recurring operations-and-maintenance work so that it no longer required the same level of active attention.
2. **Reduce platform-team overhead.** I delivered smaller initiatives that saved the platform team time and proved that the approach could create meaningful capacity.
3. **Use the capacity for customer-facing platform improvements.** With the team operating more efficiently, I developed migration plans that would improve the platform for the development teams we supported.

In parallel, I independently deepened my knowledge of the technology stack so I could design and execute the work with confidence. I did not treat ticket throughput as the end goal; I treated it as the first constraint to remove so the platform team could deliver broader value.

I respectfully disagreed with the conclusion that the migration should remain out of reach indefinitely. I did not try to force a large project ahead of the immediate KTLO priority. Instead, I committed to solving the capacity problem first and used the results of each phase to make the next decision easier for leadership to approve.

### Result

The approach created capacity that had not previously existed for larger work. The automated maintenance work and smaller platform improvements helped establish leadership confidence. I was then able to create migration plans, obtain the necessary approvals, and execute the migrations independently without execution issues.

## Why this story fits

- **Think Big:** Rather than optimizing only for clearing the next ticket, I set a broader direction: transform the team from reactive KTLO work into a platform team able to deliver improvements for other teams.
- **Ownership:** I created a mechanism to remove the constraints blocking the team instead of accepting the existing workload model as fixed.
- **Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit:** I challenged the idea that KTLO pressure permanently ruled out larger migrations, while committing to the immediate priority of keeping the platform operational. I built evidence through phased delivery rather than continuing to argue for a proposal the team could not yet support.

## Impact metrics

- **Capacity:** Converted recurring maintenance work into automated or background work, enabling time for developer asks and larger initiatives.
- **Operational outcome:** Cleared the service desk board for the first time in over one year. See [the maintenance automation story](sre-maintenance-automation.md).
- **Delivery outcome:** Earned approval to plan and independently execute larger platform migrations.

## Disagree and Commit answer framing

The disagreement was not whether KTLO work mattered; it was whether its current volume meant the migration could never be prioritized. I accepted the immediate decision to focus on KTLO work, then committed to a phased approach that removed the constraint and earned approval for the later migration.
