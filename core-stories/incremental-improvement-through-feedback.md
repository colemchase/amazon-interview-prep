# Using Feedback to Turn Stalled Improvements into Progress

## Leadership Principles demonstrated

- [Bias for Action](../leadership-principles/bias-for-action.md) — primary
- [Earn Trust](../leadership-principles/earn-trust.md)
- [Deliver Results](../leadership-principles/deliver-results.md)

## STAR story

### Situation

In my current role, I would identify a problem and bring a proposed fix to standup. At times, the technical lead preferred a more comprehensive solution and suggested incorporating the smaller fix into that work later. The larger solution often required more coordination and did not get scheduled immediately, which meant the smaller issue could remain unresolved and no improvement was made.

### Task

Use feedback to improve how I advocated for solutions: preserve the value of a more complete design while creating a practical path to make progress now.

### Action

My career manager gave me direct feedback: it was valuable that I came to standup with solutions, but when a more ideal solution was proposed for later, I should explicitly distinguish the ideal future state from the next useful step.

I changed how I framed these conversations. I acknowledged when the broader approach was the better long-term design, then proposed taking a safe, bounded step forward now: “Yes, ideally we can continue down that path. For now, let’s take a step in the right direction in case we otherwise take no steps at all.”

This let me fix smaller issues while documenting and backlogging the larger solution that needed additional coordination. Instead of treating the options as all-or-nothing, I made the immediate improvement and the long-term design part of the same plan.

### Result

The approach led to small issues being resolved rather than indefinitely deferred. Those small wins removed recurring problems and created more headroom for the team to take on larger initiatives. I learned to balance progress with idealism: a comprehensive solution is valuable, but it should not prevent a safe improvement that can be delivered now.

## Why this story fits

- **Bias for Action:** I created a path for safe progress instead of allowing a deferred ideal solution to block immediate improvements.
- **Earn Trust:** I accepted coaching, changed my communication, and treated the technical lead’s broader design perspective with respect.
- **Deliver Results:** I converted stalled discussions into smaller delivered improvements while keeping larger work visible and planned.
