# Delivering a Go-Live When an Integration Failed at the Last Minute

## Leadership Principles demonstrated

- [Deliver Results](../leadership-principles/deliver-results.md) — primary
- [Bias for Action](../leadership-principles/bias-for-action.md)
- [Ownership](../leadership-principles/ownership.md)

## STAR story

### Situation

I was responsible for delivering work on a go-live deadline that depended on an integration with another team. Five minutes before go-live, we discovered that the integration did not work on the other team’s side. At that point, waiting for the permanent integration fix would have put the launch at risk.

### Task

Protect the go-live deadline without pretending the failed integration was resolved, then make sure the temporary solution did not become a permanent, unowned workaround.

### Action

I quickly offered a temporary solution that allowed the launch to proceed despite the failed integration. I communicated the distinction clearly: it was a safe path to meet the immediate deadline, not a substitute for fixing the integration.

After go-live, I followed through with the other team to close out the underlying issue. I made sure the permanent integration was addressed rather than treating a successful launch as the end of the work.

### Result

We met the go-live deadline using a temporary solution that worked. The immediate risk to the launch was removed, and I followed through until the original integration issue was closed out.

## Why this story fits

- **Deliver Results:** I focused on the customer-facing deadline and found a viable path to launch when the original dependency failed.
- **Bias for Action:** With only minutes remaining, I proposed a practical response instead of waiting passively for another team’s fix.
- **Ownership:** I did not let the workaround become the finish line; I followed through on the root issue after launch.

## Details to add before the interview

- What was launching, who was affected, and why was the deadline important?
- What was the integration, and what precisely failed on the other team’s side?
- What was the temporary solution, and why was it safe enough to use?
- Who approved the temporary path and how did you communicate its limitations?
- What did you do after go-live to track, test, and close the permanent fix?
- How long did it take to close the issue, and what evidence confirmed closure?
- Did you add a runbook, test, contract, alert, or other guardrail to prevent recurrence?

## Likely follow-ups

- How did you decide the temporary solution was safe enough for go-live?
- Why could the other team’s integration problem not be fixed before launch?
- What would you have done if no safe temporary option existed?
- How did you make sure the permanent fix was prioritized after the deadline passed?
